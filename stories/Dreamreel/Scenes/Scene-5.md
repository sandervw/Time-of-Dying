# Dreamreel: Scene 5

The door opened onto grey, not darkness — the false-sun globe suspended above the central gallery having long since emptied of whatever luminance it once held, its dead face casting light that was not so much light as the negation of shadow, flat and without warmth or direction; Othelmedir's boot found the first flagstone's raised corner before his eyes had adjusted. He stumbled, yet straightened without comment and without breaking stride, his second foot already correcting for the descent his first had not anticipated, the hand at his side dropping to the rail without his needing to look for it.

Weirmoth moved along the balcony rail, one gloved hand running along the stone — cold even through the leather, the drop to the dead garden below shifting in and out of view through flat pillars as he sidled along — while Othelmedir examined the shelves. His hand moved from spine to spine and then stopped, the third shelf from the floor, second column from the corner, as though the shelf had been waiting for it. The spine gave under his fingers, soft and yielding, its cover having absorbed so many seasons of damp that it had become a texture rather than a structure. He drew it carefully free of its neighbors. The volume was heavier than one would expect from its mold-thinned cover. He opened it to a page at the middle; the ink, at least, had been preserved.

"That one actually worth reading, or is it for show?" Weirmoth had stopped at the rail, watching his companion.

"It is a treatise, Weirmoth," Othelmedir rejoined. He closed the book. "Which is to say that it repays study, and nowise the light perusal of the idle eye; and this treatise in particular — *Potions and Poisons of Eleven Reagents* — would repay more study, I deem, than the forty-odd volumes which have kept it company upon the shelf."

"The gas," said Weirmoth. "You were going to tell me how it works — I'd like to not breathe it wrong."

"The cauldron within this tower remains stoppered, and yet its elements do seep regardless; and these elements, dispersing outward into the air, are that which does engender the Dreamreel. The deaths which the villagers have dreamed are not visions, Weirmoth, but residue."

Othelmedir moved to a short spiral stair. Weirmoth followed a few steps behind. They descended to the library's lower floor. Othelmedir turned, and strode across the open central space. Weirmoth, uncertain of their direction at first, eventually perceived a door on this lowest level, half-hidden behind a shelf.

Then a low, sustained note reached them from beyond the door — the sound of a single great lung drawing breath after long stillness, coming through the chestnut planks.

Othelmedir did not move at once. He stood holding the treatise. Weirmoth stood a little farther back, in the center of the open central space below the dead sun, among the garden plots: nine small humped islands of pale, grassless soil amidst the ankle-deep sea of gas - and each island marked where the cabal had buried one of its mancers.

Othelmedir tucked the treatise under his arm and regarded Weirmoth with an unreadable expression.

"Weirmoth," he said. "Here, I deem, lies your metier."
