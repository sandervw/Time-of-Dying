# Othelmedir — Collected Dialogue

*Extracted from* Dreamreel *Scenes 1–9.*

## Scene One

> "You inquire, master bedmeister, whether I would take bed or bench. Let it be a bed, if any chamber yet remains un-tenented; and let it be the highest room you keep, for the quiet of an upper floor sits more kindly with a man whose labours are accomplished in the sidereal hours."

## Scene 2

> "If I may take up your figure of this 'worry', master bedmeister, I would observe that a worry ceases once the worrier sinks into sleep, whereas a Dreamreel ceases nowise; and it is this, more than the visions themselves, more than whatever dead faces crowd the shut eyelids, which lays thy township bare to ruin."

> "Othelmedir is the name I carry on these roads. I am a humble practitioner of the dream-mancer's discipline, lately come up out of the south, and at your service. My art wants for licence in these years, the last who held such warrant having died and left no heirs to keep it; yet the knowledge did not perish. I take no coin; I work by barter and by oath, though such negotiations may bide until the slate-coloured rays of the morning shine."

## Scene 3

> "Weirmoth, though thy iron has its uses, not all that which exists beyond this door may be exorcised with such mundane implements."

> "I do not carry what I cannot use."

> "Should we suffer what abides within to continue unchecked, the folk of the valley below will, ere the next bright-burning flight of the Cracked Comet is upon them, be no longer themselves; for the dead supplant the living from within, memory by memory. Two cabals contended within this tower once, the Red Pew and the Milkmoot; and it is the Red Pew, the older and more imaginative of the two, which dreams now in the beds of Sulky Wend."

> "You professed to me, and with no small vehemence, that you never dreamed at all; that within your skull there stood a winter church whose doors were barred against all comers."

> "*Öltesoldzo sjhaj*." *(In common language: 'With a sigh came loose the sutures.')*

## Scene 4

> "Walk on, good Weirmoth. The chamber that lies beyond this one, and not this one, is where the work shall be begun. Speak less, I pray you; for this air, unbreathed through so many a year, and passed only through the lungs of the dreaming dead, bears little love, I deem, for the speech of the living."

> "You owe unto yourself the next step, Weirmoth, and after that step the step which follows thereafter, and thus doth the work proceed, without the burden of further converse."

> "A token, borne of a quorum whereof I have read in divers ledgers. Let us tarry no longer, but proceed."

> "From this side it shall not be opened, Weirmoth, neither by any spear of yours, nor yet by any blade which I expect we shall espy within such a house as this."

## Scene 5

> "It is a treatise, Weirmoth. Which is to say that it repays study, and nowise the light perusal of the idle eye; and this treatise in particular — *Potions and Poisons of Eleven Reagents* — would repay more study, I deem, than the most part of those volumes which have kept it company upon the shelf."

> "The cauldron within this tower remains stoppered, and yet its elements do seep regardless, even as a sealed vessel of brine will sweat its salt through the seams; and these elements, dispersing outward into the air, are that which does engender the Dreamreel. The deaths which the villagers have dreamed are not visions, Weirmoth, but residue."

> "Weirmoth. Here, I deem, lies your metier."

## Scene 6

> "The crystals purchase nothing wherein your interest lies, Weirmoth. The brew that stands before us is the article of our covenant; that which travels in my coat is but severance laid against the long road home, and your coin, I assure you, is no thinner for it."

> "Mancer's pockets get deep when no one is counting; just so, and well said. Count, then. When the cauldron is split asunder, you may search me to the very lining of my coat. Yet for the present, attend. There is an eleventh reagent which the treatise names: *Respiration from a Silver Dream* — a breath drawn forth from a sleeper who has died within the sleep itself. The gas of this hall has preserved such a breath; for the first death it took, that of some nameless trespasser, hangs yet upon the air. To fetch it I must enter into the dream which holds it, and that I cannot do. Yet look here, and behold this second decoction which I have drawn from the store. This is the witness-draught. A practitioner habituated unto necromancy who drinks of the witness-draught burns at the nerves, and is rendered unto ash. An unhabituated man passes through it, and wakes."

> "Drink, master Weirmoth. You shall return, and with no ill savour lingering upon your lips; and together we shall go down unto the cauldron, through the wall, bearing my poison rendered from the eleventh reagent."

## Scene 7

> "Friend Weirmoth. You have slept the heavy sleep; now, ere the savor of it departs you, it would profit us both that you impart to me the substance of what visions came in that dream."

## Scene 8

> "Friend Weirmoth, the way, that has stood sealed against us, shall stand open within the hour. Thereafter we must descend together through the halls remaining, down to the cauldron chamber, and you must go before me and ward this body of mine against whatsoever waits."

> "Yonder, upon the right."

> "Friend Weirmoth, the door that follows - pass within it warily, and keep close along the wall once you are through. A ritual circle lies beyond, scored in chalk; and upon that chalk we must on no account set foot."

> "Fellow Sourbell. You speak in error. The lever was the order rendered. You gave it, and that order, being given, was obeyed."

> "Undo that one ere he rouses his wasps. His hands you must unmake before all else."

> "Kciddil, Kciddil, Kciddil." *(deadspeech — the inverse of the name)*

> "Friend Weirmoth. The way lies open below, and we must now go down to it."

> "That is a name I wear no longer."

> "Friend Weirmoth, keep what ledger you will, and enter therein what entries please you. There remains to us business yet unfinished within this tower."

## Scene 9

> "Friend Weirmoth. You have had a name out of two dead mouths this night, and you stand now with steel set betwixt us; and therefore I will lay the matter before you plainly, and lay it but once. The cabal of mancers that wrought this cauldron wrought no execrable deed; the gas lay bound within the vessel against the appointed hour of its opening, and the breaching of vessel and of hour alike was the doing of a rival house, and of no other. The dolorous plight that came upon the townfolk of Sulky Wend followed from that breach, and from no design of mine, nor of my Fellows. The silence I kept, I kept because this country - and many a country besides, it may be - has no patience for the finer turns of such a tale, and a man accused unjustly is oftentimes a man unjustly hanged. To the cowardice of that silence I confess, friend Weirmoth, and to naught beyond it; and the fruits of that cowardice I have, in this hour and within this chamber, redressed."

> "Ülledéék zárréenk. Ülledéék zárréenk." *(deadspeech — Ülledéék's Closure-Song)*

> "It shall be as you ask, master Weirmoth."

> "Master Weirmoth, you drive your bargain after the manner of a mancer."
